# Priority user flows

These flows guide information architecture and batch selection. Detailed
behavior belongs in active feature specs.

## Teacher: audit and improve a course

1. Open the tool from the current Canvas course.
2. Confirm the exact course automatically and see current course health and
   highest-impact issues without repeating connection setup.
3. Open a finding and inspect objective, evidence, assessment, and confidence.
4. Accept, reject, or defer the finding.
5. Generate or edit a remediation draft.
6. Preview a change package without publishing.
7. Compare course health after a later revision.

Success: the teacher can act without understanding retrieval or integration
internals and never wonders which Canvas course will be affected.

## Student: understand a topic

1. Open `Помощник курса` from the current Canvas course.
2. See the exact course name, the familiar ordered course map, and at most three
   plain actions: `Продолжить по курсу`, `Спросить по материалам`, and
   `Открыть источник` when a source is selected.
3. Choose a module/material or ask a question in natural language.
4. Receive a cited explanation or a clear insufficient-context response.
5. Open the cited Canvas page, file, assignment, or clearly marked external
   source.
6. Request a simpler explanation, hint, example, or self-check.
7. Mark the response helpful or report a problem.

Success: assistance improves understanding without exposing hidden materials or
delivering unrestricted graded solutions. Within one screen the learner can say
which course is open, where to continue, and what evidence the assistant uses.

## Student: decide what needs attention across courses

This follows the current-course pilot and requires a separately approved user or
global navigation placement.

1. Open the personal companion from Canvas.
2. See bounded upcoming work and recently used courses, not a ranking or risk
   score.
3. Select one course and land in its exact course map.
4. Return to the authoritative Canvas source for deadlines and submission.

Success: a learner with many Canvas courses can choose the next action without
the product becoming a second gradebook or surveillance surface.

## Methodologist: inspect course quality

1. Open the assigned course or review queue.
2. Filter by unsupported objective, assessment gap, Bloom mismatch, or low
   confidence.
3. Inspect evidence and teacher review history.
4. Record a methodological decision or request revision.
5. Export a reproducible evidence package.

Success: review is traceable and does not rely on an opaque score.

## Program designer: trace competency coverage

1. Select a program outcome or competency.
2. See courses that introduce, develop, and assess it.
3. Identify gaps, duplication, prerequisite breaks, and cognitive progression.
4. Drill down to course evidence.
5. model a proposed change and inspect affected courses.

Success: the program is understandable as a connected learning system.

## Administrator: monitor product and aggregate quality

1. Select an organization or program.
2. View aggregate adoption, unresolved gaps, latency, and cost.
3. Separate operational incidents from educational findings.
4. Drill down only when role and purpose permit.

Success: decisions use aggregate evidence without turning the product into
surveillance.
