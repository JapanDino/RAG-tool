# Product experience

## Design thesis

The interface should feel like a clear, living map of learning rather than an
AI console. Users work with recognizable educational objects: goals, lessons,
assignments, evidence, questions, competencies, and revisions.

The visual signature is the Workshop Course Route:

    Objective -------- Material -------- Assessment
        filled link: supported
        broken link: gap
        dotted link: uncertain

The route is a functional navigation and explanation device, not decoration.
It may appear in findings, tutor citations, course health, and program maps.
Grid paper is the common working surface; mint, yellow, and apricot notes mark
evidence, review, and current position. Tape, rotation, and offset shadows are
reserved for those meaningful anchors, never repeated on every card.

## Role-specific experiences

### Student — focused study desk

- One current question or learning task dominates the screen.
- Sources and progress are visible without competing with the explanation.
- Hints reveal progressively.
- Assessment restrictions are explained in plain language.

### Teacher — evidence workspace

- Findings are ordered by impact and uncertainty.
- Every recommendation connects to source evidence and an explicit review action.
- Advanced diagnostics use progressive disclosure.
- The default view answers “what needs my attention?”

### Methodologist and program designer — curriculum map

- Relationships across objectives, courses, competencies, and assessments are
  the primary structure.
- Aggregate signals always drill down to inspectable evidence.
- Comparison and change impact are more important than chat.

### Administrator — operational overview

- Show aggregate quality, adoption, latency, and unresolved risk.
- Avoid individual conversation feeds and opaque staff rankings.
- Separate product health from pedagogical quality.

## Interaction principles

- One primary action per state.
- Evidence is one click away and never hidden behind technical terminology.
- Confidence is expressed as actionable language plus detail, not a decorative
  percentage alone.
- Destructive or consequential actions require preview and confirmation.
- Empty and failure states explain the next valid action.
- Motion communicates state transitions and respects reduced-motion settings.

## Content principles

- Write from the user’s side of the screen.
- Prefer “Check course” over “Run pipeline”.
- Prefer “Sources” over “Retrieved chunks”.
- Keep the same action name through button, progress, and result messages.
- Errors state what failed, what was preserved, and what the user can do next.
